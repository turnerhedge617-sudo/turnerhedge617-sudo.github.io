About me

A practice-informed perspective on social challenges, community needs, and inclusive development.

My academic and field experiences have shaped my interest in how social programmes, public services, and community-based initiatives can expand opportunity and improve well-being.

During my undergraduate studies in Social Work, I explored women’s economic empowerment through the role of Industrial Homes (Sanatzar). In the field, I engaged with rural families around educational access and helped connect families with relevant support. These experiences strengthened my interest in research that listens to people’s lived experiences and informs practical responses.

I hope to develop stronger research and policy skills through further study and contribute to evidence-informed social development.

Undergraduate thesis

The Role of Industrial Homes (Sanatzar) in the Economic Empowerment of Women

This project examined community-based skill development initiatives and their relationship to women’s economic empowerment, financial independence, economic participation, and social inclusion. It informed my continuing interest in how local programmes can support women’s agency and livelihoods.

2021 – 2022

Field Agent / Intern · National Commission for Human Development (NCHD)

Sargodha, Pakistan

Engaged with rural households where low literacy and children being out of school were concerns. Conducted door-to-door outreach, spoke with parents about barriers to school attendance, including financial constraints and limited awareness, and assisted families with school enrolment and access to information about government scholarships or financial support. The work required listening, trust-building, community communication, and connecting families with public services.
